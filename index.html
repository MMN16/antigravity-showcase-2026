Act I: Behavioral Biology

The Survival Instinct: Fear & The Great Divergence (2020)

When existential panic strikes, human psychology strips away all social signaling. In Q2 2020 (Circuit Breaker), the Singapore consumer economy split into two opposite realities: Supermarkets & Hypermarkets surged by +53.9% as residents stockpiled rice and toilet paper, while Watches & Jewellery crashed -82.0% and Wearing Apparel fell -81.5%. Social ego died; primal survival prevailed.

Act II: Compensatory Psychology

The Dopamine Snapback: The +615% Revenge Supercycle

Behavioral economics defines compensatory consumption as spending triggered by feelings of psychological deprivation. Once border restrictions and dining bans eased, Singapore experienced the fiercest Veblen luxury flex in its modern history: Watches & Jewellery rose from an index low of 19.27 to a historic high of 137.88 (+615.5%) by Q4 2022, staying elevated through 2025 at an astonishing 170.41.

Act III: Micro-Indulgence Theory

The Lipstick Paradox: Small Dopamine When Big Dreams Stall

Leonard Lauder coined the Lipstick Index during the 2001 recession: when macro uncertainty or high inflation prices consumers out of major capital commitments (houses, cars, luxury holidays), they compensate with affordable luxuries (cosmetics, premium coffee, artisanal treats). Looking across 40 years (1997 AFC, 2003 SARS, 2008 GFC, 2020 COVID), Cosmetics & Medical Goods never suffered catastrophic downturns—it consistently acts as the emotional stabilizer of the Singapore wallet.
